No practice car?
Keep your learning moving.

Access to a car and suitable insurance can make private driving practice difficult. An online simulator gives you a place to work on road awareness at home, without driving a real vehicle.

01

What you can practise without a real car

You can study the Highway Code, revisit signs and road priorities, and think through junctions and crossings. A simulator adds a visual setting in which to explore those decisions.

In SlayDrive, you can practise with road scenarios and simulator-style mock tests, then review feedback. These activities support road awareness; they do not teach every physical skill involved in driving a car.

02

Do you need insurance for a driving simulator?

You do not need motor insurance to use an on-screen simulator because you are not operating a real vehicle. A simulator subscription is not an insurance policy and does not give you cover for a car.

If learner insurance is expensive, unavailable or unclear, you can use screen-based practice while arranging suitable real-world practice. SlayDrive does not resolve an insurance refusal or provide advice on whether an insurer will cover you.

03

Before you practise on a public road

Real-road learning has separate requirements, including the appropriate provisional licence, suitable supervision and valid insurance. The vehicle must also meet the applicable legal requirements.

Check current GOV.UK guidance and speak to the insurer about the exact vehicle, learner and practice arrangement. Do not assume someone else’s policy covers you, or that a simulator result changes the requirements.

04

Build a useful learning habit at home

Choose one topic, read the official rule, explore it in the simulator and note a question for your next lesson. For example, consider who has priority at a crossing and what you would need to observe before moving.
