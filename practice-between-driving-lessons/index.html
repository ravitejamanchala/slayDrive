01

An alternative to extra classes for road-awareness practice

Driving lessons teach practical skills in a real vehicle, with an instructor who can observe and guide you. A simulator is different: it gives you a place to explore road layouts, notice hazards and revisit decisions on screen.

If you are searching for an alternative to driving classes, start by separating road-knowledge practice from learning to control a real car. SlayDrive supports the first part; it does not replace the second.

02

A short routine between lessons

After a lesson, write down one situation you found difficult: giving way, a crossing, or spotting another road user. Read the relevant Highway Code guidance, then explore a related situation in the simulator.

Pause after a decision and ask what you noticed, what you missed and which rule applies. Bring any uncertainty to your instructor rather than treating simulator feedback as proof that you have mastered a real-road skill.

03

When private practice is difficult

Not everyone has access to a suitable car, a supervisor or affordable learner insurance. On-screen practice lets you continue learning about road decisions without arranging a vehicle for that session.

SlayDrive includes simulator-style mock tests, location maps, Highway Code learning and puzzles. The subscription costs £4.99 per month; it is separate from the cost of driving lessons or insurance.

04

What still needs real driving practice

A screen cannot reproduce the full experience of steering, braking, clutch control, observation and responding to real traffic. Arrange practical lessons and appropriately supervised practice to develop those skills.

Simulator results do not count as official driving tests, guarantee a pass or certify readiness for the road. Follow current GOV.UK guidance and your instructor’s advice.
